About Us

More than four years. A closer look at the four ideas that shape the Zeta Kappa experience: brotherhood, service, leadership, and a connection that continues beyond college.

Our story

What does being a Theta Chi here actually mean?

This page is being built to tell that story the same way members experience it—through the people, the work, the memories, and the responsibilities that accumulate over time.

01 · BROTHERHOOD

A place to belong—and contribute.

Brotherhood is more than living near the same people or wearing the same letters. It is built through the everyday moments: meals, late nights, chapter projects, road trips, difficult conversations, celebrations, and the expectation that brothers show up for one another.

The goal is not to make everyone the same. It is to create a chapter where different personalities, majors, backgrounds, and interests can become part of one shared experience.

02 · SERVICE

Do something useful with the group around you.

Service gives the chapter a reason to look beyond itself. Zeta Kappa members contribute time and effort to projects that support the campus, the surrounding community, and causes our members care about.

The value is not just the number of hours completed. It is learning to notice where help is needed, organize people around a task, and leave something better than we found it.

03 · LEADERSHIP

Learn by being trusted with something real.

A fraternity gives students opportunities to make decisions that have consequences. Members can lead committees, manage events, organize recruitment, coordinate service, communicate with alumni, oversee budgets, and help determine the direction of the chapter.

Those responsibilities create room to learn how to communicate, plan ahead, recover from mistakes, work with people who disagree, and finish what you started.

04 · BEYOND COLLEGE

The chapter has a memory longer than any one class.

Zeta Kappa has existed at Ohio Northern University since 1966. Every active chapter inherits something built by the members before it, and each graduating class becomes part of the alumni community that follows.

That continuity gives members a connection to people they may never have attended school with, while alumni, advisors, and chapter leaders help preserve knowledge and support the organization across generations.
